Published Work · Books

Three books, and one
stubborn question.

Fifteen years apart in publication and further apart in subject, but circling the same thing: what happens when the floor you have been standing on turns out to be one more layer waiting to be peeled. One on building software, two on how to think about what is actually there.

The connective tissue is not incidental. The design philosophy running through the articles — that every design is a throwaway design, that there is no final state, that reality always bats last — is the same argument the newest book makes at full length.

Nothing Holds: How to Understand Reality

The sun does not rise — every eye on the planet reports otherwise, and every eye is mistaken. That gap between what intuition insists on and what evidence establishes is where the book starts, before turning on a stranger assumption: that causes only ever flow upward, from particles to molecules to minds, and never back down. No experiment ever delivered that rule. It arrived as a laboratory habit and hardened into doctrine. To audit it, Alex seats an improbable pair of reviewers side by side — Peter Atkins, who derives the laws of nature from the properties of nothing, and Nagarjuna, who demonstrated eighteen centuries ago that no phenomenon holds an essence of its own, nothing included. Along the way: a traffic jam that shoves cars around while consisting of nothing but cars, Maxwell's demon undone by the cost of his own memory, and a dying man refusing treatment until somebody tells him the arrow's exact velocity. No prior acquaintance with Madhyamaka philosophy or quantum mechanics assumed.

Artisan Software Development for the 21st Century

Hands-on instruction in building artisan-grade software for the web: robust, resilient REST APIs built on HATEOAS, the switch from class-oriented to genuinely object-oriented programming, and the top-down, outside-in approach to design. Turns on a distinction worth the price by itself — between solutions with no obvious deficiencies and solutions that obviously have no deficiencies. All exercises are worked in Ruby on Rails.

Difficult Points In Buddhism

Buddhism as a system of philosophical thought that eschews metaphysics, staying inside the range of experience when establishing what is true about the world and our place in it. The book takes up the harder points a practitioner meets along the path, with particular attention to the facets of Buddhism as practised today that were never part of the original teachings.
