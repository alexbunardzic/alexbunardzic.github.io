Conference Talks · 1 session

Recorded on the main circuit, in front of a room.

October 10, 2025 · Devoxx · 67 min

The Future of Refactoring: Test-Driven Navigation

AI promised to take refactoring off our hands and instead delivers sweeping, uncontrolled changes. Test-Driven Navigation is the workflow that tames it: failing tests act as hyper-specific instructions that force the assistant into safe, incremental steps. Combines XP's three C's with Permaculture by Succession, includes a live-coded red-green-refactor cycle with an AI partner, and reframes code review — writing the next test is the review. The closing argument is that the developer's role is not obsolete but elevated: mechanic to race engineer, coder to architect.

Talks and Interviews · 4 sessions

The 2016—2017 run on conversational commerce and bots for business — a conference talk, a meetup, an academy session, and an interview, recorded while the first wave of chatbots was arriving.

May 25, 2017 · Vancouver Ruby Meetup · 86 min

Building Chatbots for Business

Chatbots at the time were aimed at individual users, and the NLP of the day could not handle free-form chat predictably — which made open-ended conversation a risky thing to offer as a service. Turns the problem around: design the communication protocol that serves the business rather than the open conversation that serves the individual. Works through four types of communication protocol and how choosing the right one produces predictable, consistent customer service.

October 14, 2016 · ChatbotConf, Vienna · 24 min

How to avoid a Pyramid of Doom when designing Bot Workflows

A conversational workflow branches on every reply, and writing those branches as nested conditionals produces the pyramid of doom — the deeply indented structure that makes a bot impossible to change later. On designing the workflow so the nesting never builds up. Presented at ChatbotConf 2016, hosted by oratio.

August 3, 2016 · DojoLIVE! · 67 min

Are You Ready For The Bot Disruption?

A DojoLIVE! interview, an hour on what the arrival of conversational interfaces meant for businesses — what bots were and were not ready to do in 2016, and what it would take to put one in front of customers.

February 2016 · RED Academy, Vancouver · 47 min

Breaking the Fourth Wall in Software

An invited session at RED Academy on conversational commerce: why people were moving off browsers and mobile apps and into messaging channels, getting things done by chatting — with friends, with coworkers, and with third-party software. Closes with a demonstration of building a conversational bot. The deck and the essay of the same name are both on this site.

Diary of a Perplexed Software Developer · 15 sessions

Fifteen short entries recorded over eight weeks in the summer of 2023, delivered straight to camera with no script. Each one picks up a habit in our industry that Alex finds baffling and turns it over for a few minutes. Listed in viewing order, day 1 through day 15.

July 14, 2023 · 3 min

Day 1

The opener, three minutes long. Alex sets out the premise — a long list of habits in our industry that leave him baffled — and promises to keep going through it.

July 15, 2023 · 5 min

Day 2

Why do we regard a software product as just another manufactured article? Software is nothing like a chair or a bridge, and the industry's insistence that it is leaves Alex genuinely baffled.

July 16, 2023 · 4 min

Day 3

Recorded on a sunny afternoon, on the walk back from a record store. Software is eating the world and the creation has outgrown its creators — what puzzles Alex is how many of us still deny it. His advice: quit the denial and enjoy the ride.

July 17, 2023 · 6 min

Day 4

Developers vanish into a large batch and resurface months later with something that misses the mark — ninety-nine out of a hundred times, in Alex's experience. Nobody can picture the finished product that far ahead, which is why feedback has to arrive while the software is being built, not after it has been delivered.

July 18, 2023 · 6 min

Day 5

Software development is still treated as a solitary activity, which Alex finds odd given that nobody seriously claims good software comes out of one head working alone.

July 20, 2023 · 4 min

Day 6

The burn-the-toast-and-you-scrape-it model: one group ships something half-baked, another group inspects it afterwards, and both sit inside the same organization. Alex argues for bringing everyone whose knowledge the job requires into the room at the outset. Every staggered alternative is pure waste, and riskier besides.

July 23, 2023 · 7 min

Day 7

Filmed after a weekend of illness. Back to the question raised on day 5, pressed harder: why do so many developers actively refuse the team and demand to be left alone with the code?

July 27, 2023 · 6 min

Day 8

Every liberating invention turns into machinery we serve rather than machinery that serves us, until we are hamsters in the wheel. Alex's proposal is to walk away from the wheel and hold technology to its original bargain.

July 28, 2023 · 9 min

Day 9

A detour away from the industry. Our thirst for knowledge is bottomless, Alex observes, and we aim nearly all of it outward — at the world, almost never at ourselves.

August 1, 2023 · 7 min

Day 10

Writers have sophisticated tools at their disposal; developers have something closer to flint and stone. Alex cannot understand who or what keeps our tools and processes so primitive, and the comparison is meant to sting.

August 1, 2023 · 7 min

Day 11

We take a snapshot of the product, squirrel it away, and work on it alone in secrecy, as though we were ashamed of what we are doing. Everyone else on the team does the same, then we try to put it all back together and absorb the inevitable clashes. Alex asks why we punish ourselves like this.

August 4, 2023 · 8 min

Day 12

Organizations keep optimizing for utilization — everybody busy, every hour booked — instead of optimizing for value delivery. Alex's disappointment on this one is undisguised.

August 8, 2023 · 9 min

Day 13

Why do we choose the most brittle approach available, every single time? Much better ways to build robust systems exist, Alex notes, yet almost no developer has heard of them and fewer still show any interest in learning them.

August 17, 2023 · 6 min

Day 14

Why do we judge a software product by its behaviour alone? If it works the way the demo promised, we declare it good to go — worth releasing, worth paying for. Alex wants a closer look at what the demo hides: quality design, clean code, solid security, performance that scales under load. Almost no buyer ever asks.

September 9, 2023 · 10 min

Day 15

The final entry, and the sharpest. Organizations announce they are agile and then fail at it, because real agility means embracing any change that comes, not picking and choosing which changes you are willing to accept.

All fifteen entries are collected in the Diary of a Perplexed Software Developer playlist on YouTube.

AI Craftspeople Guild Core Team discussion on why we want AI agents to write the failing test first

The mob halts mid-session on a question about sequencing: when we say the test has to fail, do we mean the old TDD cycle, or a test that fails against a system already running? The answer turns on a second reason — an agent is aimed at a passing run, and given a loose instruction it will get there by editing the expectation rather than the logic. Hence mutation testing as the standing audit. Written up in full as A green build is no longer evidence.
